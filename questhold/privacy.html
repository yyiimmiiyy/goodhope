Privacy Policy — QuestHold AI

Last updated: October 27, 2026

Provider: Goodhope Technologies LLC ("we", "us"), United States. Email: support@goodhopetechnologies.com. Our business address and phone number are shown on QuestHold's Google Play and App Store listings.

This policy covers QuestHold on Android phones and on iPhones (and iPads). Where the two work differently, it says so: "On Android" or "On an iPhone".

In short

  • QuestHold has no accounts and no servers of its own. We do not collect your personal information, and nothing you type or say is sent to us.
  • The AI Game Master runs on your phone. Your worlds are stored on your phone, encrypted.
  • Google Play Games (on Android): while you are signed in, each deed you earn is also unlocked in your own Play Games account, and if you turn on Cloud save, a copy of each world you play goes to that account too. It goes to your Google account, never to us. On an iPhone, deeds unlock in your own Game Center account instead, and there is no Cloud save (section 7).
  • QuestHold uses the internet to download its AI models, and to open a link or an email when you choose to. Google Play also checks your purchase and, where the law requires it, your age group. On an iPhone, the App Store checks your purchase, and Apple tells QuestHold your age range only if you choose to share it (section 6).
  • If you play co-op with friends, the phones at the table talk to each other over your Wi-Fi or hotspot. That goes from phone to phone, never to us.
  • No ads. QuestHold itself has no analytics, no tracking and no crash reports. On Android, the Google Play libraries inside the app (for purchases, Play Games and the model delivery) keep their own technical diagnostics with Google, under Google's privacy policy (sections 2 and 7). On an iPhone, the App Store and Game Center are Apple's services, under Apple's privacy policy.

1. What stays on your phone

All of this is kept on your phone and is never sent to us. On an iPhone, iOS also puts QuestHold's files and settings (apart from the AI models) in your iPhone's own backup, if you use iCloud Backup or back up to a computer (section 12).

  • Your worlds: their places and people, your heroes, characters, the story so far, every dice roll and the chronicle. They are kept in an encrypted database (SQLCipher, AES-256) whose key is held in Android's secure key store on this phone. On an iPhone, the key is held in the iPhone's Keychain, Apple's secure store for keys.
  • What you type and say: your moves and anything else you write. In single-player, it leaves the phone only if you turn on Cloud save, which copies your worlds, moves included, to your own Google account (section 7). Cloud save is on Android only.
  • Your voice: if you play by voice, your speech is turned into text on the phone. See section 3.
  • Your voiceprint: if you play by voice, QuestHold learns your voice from your first spoken moves (or from three short lines you choose to say in Settings > Voice), so that at a shared table it can leave other voices, and a TV, out. It keeps a few numbers that describe your voice (its pitch and the shape of its sound), never a recording, only on this phone. QuestHold never sends them anywhere, not even to the other phones at your table. Turn this off or delete it at any time in Settings > Voice > Know my voice; uninstalling QuestHold deletes it too.
  • Your spoken notes: if you record a spoken note (on a place on the map, a person, or the Chronicle), the recording of your voice is kept on this phone, in QuestHold's own folder, with the words it says, so you can find it again. It goes into a backup file only if you make one, and on an iPhone into the iPhone's own backup if you use one (section 12). It never goes to the other phones at your table, into Cloud save, or to us. Delete a note in its list, or delete the world, and the recording is deleted.
  • Pictures: the pictures QuestHold paints are made on the phone, and a safety filter on the phone checks each one before it is shown.
  • Settings: for example whether you roll your own dice, whether downloads wait for Wi-Fi, which version of the Terms you agreed to, which passages and pictures you reported, and your free-turn count (on Android, two numbers about your free trial are also kept by Google Play services: section 12).
  • Your age group from Google Play, or on an iPhone your age range from Apple, if they give one (section 6).
  • The AI model files the app downloaded, or, on Android, Google Play installed with it.
  • A short error log kept by the phone's own system log (Android's, or on an iPhone, iOS's), with personal details removed. QuestHold never sends it anywhere. If you chose, in your phone's settings, to share analytics with app developers (on an iPhone: Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements), Google or Apple may give us reports of QuestHold crashing: where in QuestHold's code it stopped, the phone model and the system version. They never hold your worlds or what you typed.

2. When QuestHold uses the internet

The model download

If you installed QuestHold from Google Play, Google Play brings the story model with the app, and, on phones whose AI chip QuestHold paints with, the art models (see "Models that come with the app" below). On an iPhone, the App Store brings only the app itself. Apart from that, nothing downloads until you agree. Then QuestHold fetches only the models your phone can run, from these places:

  • Story model (Gemma 4 E2B by Google): from Hugging Face, huggingface.co/unsloth/gemma-4-E2B-it-GGUF, only when Google Play did not bring it with the app (for example, an app not installed from Google Play, and always on an iPhone).
  • Story model for the graphics chip (Android only; the same Gemma 4 E2B, as Google's LiteRT-LM file): from Hugging Face, huggingface.co/litert-community/gemma-4-E2B-it-litert-lm, only on phones whose graphics chip can run it, only after the story model already works on the phone and the other downloads are in, and only over Wi-Fi. If the phone's own test finds it is not faster there, the file is deleted. An iPhone never downloads this file: where its graphics chip is faster, it runs the story model above there.
  • Memory model (EmbeddingGemma 2 by Google): from Hugging Face, the files Google publishes as litert-community/embeddinggemma-2-740m-litert-lm, -text-vision-440m-litert-lm or -text-270m-litert-lm (the one this phone's kind uses).
  • Voices and speech recognition (Kokoro by hexgrad, with its Spanish voices, its pronunciation lists, Supertonic 2 by Supertone, Moonshine by Useful Sensors, Whisper by OpenAI for listening in Spanish, and Silero VAD): from Hugging Face (huggingface.co/onnx-community/Kokoro-82M-v1.0-ONNX, huggingface.co/Supertone/supertonic-2, huggingface.co/PeterReid/graphemes_to_phonemes_en_us, huggingface.co/moonshine-ai/moonshine, huggingface.co/ggerganov/whisper.cpp) and GitHub (github.com/hexgrad/misaki, github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx for the Silero VAD file).
  • Picture safety filter: from Hugging Face, huggingface.co/onnx-community/nsfw_image_detection-ONNX.
  • Art models, on phones that paint pictures on the processor, and on iPhones that paint pictures (Stable Diffusion 1.5, TCD-LoRA and TAESD): from Hugging Face (huggingface.co/second-state/stable-diffusion-v1-5-GGUF, huggingface.co/h1t/TCD-SD15-LoRA, huggingface.co/madebyollin/taesd). Phones that downloaded art before October 2026 also keep LCM-LoRA (huggingface.co/latent-consistency/lcm-lora-sdv1-5). QuestHold's own painting style (the Keep style LoRA) comes inside the app and downloads nothing.
  • The hero portrait's depth and repainting (Depth Anything V2 Small and TAESD's encoder): from Hugging Face (huggingface.co/onnx-community/depth-anything-v2-small, huggingface.co/julienkay/taesd).
  • Art models for phones with an AI chip (Android only): delivered by Google Play with the app.

Like any download, these requests show Hugging Face, GitHub, Google Play and the content-delivery networks they use your IP address, the file being fetched and basic details of the app making the request. QuestHold sends no name, account, identifier or game data with them. Their own privacy policies apply: Hugging Face, GitHub, Google.

On Android, while the download runs, Android shows a notification so it can carry on when you leave the app. If Android stops a long download, QuestHold carries on with it the next time you open the app. On an iPhone, QuestHold asks iOS to carry on with the download while you are in another app, and iOS asks you once whether QuestHold may show notifications about it. If a download stops, QuestHold carries on with it the next time you open the app.

Models that come with the app (Android)

When you install or update QuestHold from Google Play, Google Play also downloads the story model (Gemma 4 E2B, 3.1 GB, in three parts) and, on phones with a supported AI chip, the art models for that chip, using Google Play's "Play for On-device AI". Only model files are ever shared between the apps, never your worlds or anything you typed. On an iPhone, each app keeps its own copy.

3. Your voice and the AI voices

  • Playing as a guest: a phone that cannot run the AI plays only at a friend's table. The AI then runs on the host's phone, nothing is downloaded to the guest's phone, and the guest's moves go only to the phones at that table (section 5).
  • Speaking your moves: if you choose to play by voice, your phone asks you for the microphone first (an iPhone also asks for speech recognition). Your speech is turned into text on the phone, either by QuestHold's own speech model (Moonshine, or Whisper when you play in Spanish) or by your phone's own speech recognition, depending on what this phone has ready and does best; QuestHold always asks the phone's recognition to work on the phone only. The audio is not sent anywhere or kept (only the voiceprint numbers in section 1, if you leave that on, and a spoken note you choose to record). On Android, the phone's recognition is part of Android (usually provided by Google) and works under its own privacy terms. On an iPhone, it is Apple's speech recognition, which QuestHold uses only where it can run on the iPhone itself, so your audio is not sent to Apple. We never receive your audio.
  • The voices you hear are made by AI on your phone (Kokoro or Supertonic 2), or by your phone's own text-to-speech before the voices have downloaded. They are not recordings of real people.

4. Smart dice (Bluetooth)

If you choose to connect a smart die (such as Pixels dice), QuestHold uses Bluetooth to find the die and read the number it rolls. Android asks you for the "Nearby devices" permission first. On Android 11 and older, Android calls that permission "Location", because on those versions any Bluetooth scan could hint at where you are; QuestHold asks for it only to find your die, and never reads or keeps your location. On an iPhone, iOS asks you for Bluetooth first, and QuestHold never asks for your location. The connection is between your phone and your die only; nothing about it is sent to us.

5. Co-op on the same Wi-Fi or hotspot

Co-op works without the internet and without our servers. The phone that hosts the game runs the story; friends' phones join it over the same Wi-Fi network or the host's phone hotspot.

  • While a table has a free seat, the host's phone announces it on the local network every two seconds (UDP port 42021), so friends' phones can find it: a short table label the host chose (the host's hero's name unless they change it), how many seats are free, the port to connect to, and a random number for that table. Any device on the same network can see that announcement. It never carries the world's title, the story or anyone's moves.
  • To sit down, a friend's phone connects to the host's phone (TCP port 42020) and sends the four-digit table code shown on the host, and a random secret key that QuestHold made on the friend's phone, so a returning friend gets their own hero back. Nothing else is accepted before the code is right, and wrong guesses are limited.
  • Once seated, the friend's phone sends their hero (name, abilities and training), their moves (typed, or spoken and turned into text on their phone), the dice they roll, and their level-up choices. The host's phone sends each friend the story the Game Master tells, the heroes at the table and the scene, and asks them to roll when the game needs it.
  • Who receives it: only the phones at that table, directly over your local network. None of it goes to us or over the internet.
  • How it travels: as plain messages on your local network, not encrypted. Anyone else on the same network could see them if they tried, so play co-op on a network you trust, such as your home Wi-Fi or your own phone's hotspot, not public Wi-Fi.
  • What is kept: the world, including each friend's hero and their secret key, is saved only on the host's phone, in its encrypted database. A friend's phone keeps its own secret key and table settings.
  • On newer Android versions, Android asks you before QuestHold may use the local network. On an iPhone, iOS always asks you first. iOS may also keep an iPhone from sending or hearing the announcements in this section; a friend can then sit down from the table's QR code or invite link, or by typing its address.

Sending a world to a friend. You can send one of your worlds (as it was built, or as it stands, with your story so far, if you choose) to a friend:

  • To a phone nearby: while your phone offers the world, it announces it on the local network every two seconds (UDP port 42023): the world's title, the port to connect to and a random number. Any device on the same network can see that announcement. Your friend's phone connects to yours (TCP port 42022) with the four-digit code shown on your phone, and the world goes straight to it, checked so that nothing changed on the way. It travels as plain messages on your local network, like co-op. Leaving the screen stops offering it.
  • As a file: your phone's share sheet hands a world file to the app you pick (a message, an email, a drive). From there it is that app's to handle, under its own privacy policy.
  • What a world holds: its title, its places, people, words, laws and where its story starts, and a painting of it if your phone painted one. Never your hero, your story, your storyteller settings or your spoken notes, unless you choose to send the world as it stands, which holds your hero and the story so far (still never your spoken notes). It never goes to us.

Invite links and QR codes. A table's or a world's link (https://goodhopetechnologies.com/questhold/join/…) carries the host phone's address on your Wi-Fi and the four-digit code after the "#", which a browser never sends to any server. Opening it on a phone without QuestHold AI shows a page on goodhopetechnologies.com (the site's host sees the page request, as for any web page). If you tap "Get QuestHold AI on Google Play" there, the address and code go to Google Play as the install referrer, and QuestHold AI reads them back on the phone once, on its first start, to take you to the table. QuestHold AI on an iPhone never reads an install referrer: there, scan the table's code or type its address inside QuestHold AI.

6. Age checks (Google Play Age Signals, and Apple's Declared Age Range on an iPhone)

Some places, such as Texas, require app stores and apps to know whether a player is a minor and to get a parent's approval for some things.

On Android, when you open QuestHold, and when you open the Pro screen, QuestHold asks Google Play's Age Signals service on the phone what it knows about the account: whether its age is shared, its age range (for example 13 to 15), and whether a parent manages it. Google Play may ask you first whether to share it. QuestHold keeps only that answer, on your phone, and uses it only to follow these laws:

  • If a parent manages the account, buying Pro goes through Google Play's parental approval, and Pro opens when the parent approves.
  • If Google Play says the account belongs to someone under 18 whom no parent manages in Google Play, Pro waits for a parent: they can buy it on their own account, or supervise yours and approve it.
  • If the law where you live requires a verified age and Google Play does not have one yet, Pro waits until you confirm your age in Google Play.
  • If Google Play says the account belongs to someone under 13, QuestHold cannot be played on it.

On an iPhone (iOS 26 or later), QuestHold asks Apple's Declared Age Range only when you open the Pro screen, never before. Apple shows its own screen, and you (or your parent) choose whether to share. If you share, QuestHold receives only an age range (for example 13 to 15) and whether a parent or guardian declared it. QuestHold keeps only that answer, on your phone, and uses it only for these rules:

  • If a parent or guardian declared the age, buying Pro asks them to approve it with Apple's Ask to Buy, and Pro opens when they do.
  • If the range is under 18 and no parent or guardian declared it, Pro waits for a parent: they can buy it on their own account, or approve it with Ask to Buy.
  • If the range is under 13, QuestHold cannot be played on that account.

Free play is never held back by these checks, and if Google Play or Apple gives no answer (or you choose not to share), QuestHold works as normal. We never receive the answer and never use it for anything else.

7. Google Play Games and Game Center: sign-in, achievements and cloud save

On Android, QuestHold works with Google Play Games, the Google service that keeps game achievements and saved games in your own Google account. If you use Google Play Games on this phone, it signs you in by itself when QuestHold opens; you can sign in, sign out or turn it off in Google Play Games.

  • Deeds (achievements): QuestHold's deeds are earned on your phone from what happens in your games, and are kept on your phone. While you are signed in to Google Play Games, each deed you earn is also unlocked in your Play Games account, where you (and anyone who can see your Play Games profile) can see it. Only which deed it is goes with it: no story, no moves and no game data.
  • Cloud save, off until you turn it on: if you turn on Cloud save in QuestHold's settings, a copy of each world you play (its places and people, the story so far and its dice rolls) is kept as a saved game in your own Play Games account, when you stop playing and when you tap Save now. It goes from your phone to your Google account: we never receive it and cannot read it. You can bring a copy back on any phone signed in to the same account. The copy is sealed so QuestHold can tell it was not changed or cut short; Google keeps it under Google's privacy policy.
  • When copies disagree: a save never replaces a copy of the same world that went further on another phone, and bringing a copy back never replaces a story on your phone: it comes in beside it. If two phones save at the same moment, Google Play keeps the one with more turns played.
  • Deleting it: turn Cloud save off in QuestHold's settings to stop saving. Copies already saved stay in your Play Games account until you delete QuestHold's data or your Play Games profile in Google Play Games' settings.

While you use it, the Play Games library inside QuestHold also sends Google its own analytics and diagnostics, which Google uses to keep its service working (developer.android.com/games/pgs/data-collection). Google Play Games is Google's service, under Google's privacy policy: Google.

On an iPhone, QuestHold works with Apple's Game Center instead. If you are signed in to Game Center on your iPhone, QuestHold is signed in when it opens; if you are not, Apple's sign-in shows only when you tap Sign in in QuestHold's settings. You can turn Game Center off in your iPhone's Settings.

  • Deeds (achievements): earned and kept on your phone, as on Android. While you are signed in, each deed you earn is also unlocked in your Game Center account, where you (and other players, as your Game Center privacy settings allow) can see it. Only which deed it is goes with it: no story, no moves and no game data.
  • Who is signed in: Game Center tells QuestHold your Game Center name, which QuestHold shows in its settings, and a player ID, which QuestHold keeps on the phone to remember which deeds it has already sent.
  • No cloud save: QuestHold copies none of your worlds to Game Center or to iCloud. To keep a copy, use "Back up and restore" (section 12).

Game Center is Apple's service, under Apple's privacy policy: Apple.

8. World news notifications, off until you turn them on

After your second session QuestHold asks you once whether you want word when your world moves; you can also turn World news on or off in its settings. If you say yes, your phone asks whether QuestHold may show notifications. Then, at most once a week, when a group in your world moves on while you are away, QuestHold shows one short notification about it. The AI Game Master writes it on your phone ahead of time and the phone schedules it: nothing is sent over the internet and nothing reaches us. It never makes a sound between 10 PM and 8 AM. On Android, the notification itself has a button that turns World news off; on an iPhone, turn it off in QuestHold's settings or in the iPhone's notification settings.

12. Keeping and deleting your data

  • Your worlds stay on your phone until you delete them. Uninstalling QuestHold, or clearing its storage in Android's settings, deletes them and everything else QuestHold stored, apart from two numbers about your free trial (below). We cannot recover them. On an iPhone, deleting the app deletes your worlds and QuestHold's files, but iOS keeps the few small entries QuestHold put in the Keychain (the database key, your Pro record, and your trial and free-turn counts), and QuestHold uses them again if you install it again.
  • On Android, so that the 7-day trial does not start over when QuestHold's storage is cleared or the app is reinstalled, QuestHold also keeps two numbers in Google Play services' Block Store: when you first opened QuestHold, and the latest time it has seen on your phone's clock. Nothing else goes there: no worlds, nothing you typed or said, no name or account details. Google Play services keeps them on your phone, where only QuestHold can read them, and, if your phone backs up to your Google account (Settings > Google > Backup), in that backup too, end-to-end encrypted when your phone has a screen lock, so they also come back when you set up a new phone from that backup. We never receive them, and you do not sign in to anything in QuestHold for this. Google keeps and deletes that backup under Google's privacy policy.
  • On Android, QuestHold does not let Android back up its files to your Google account. To keep a copy of a world, use "Back up and restore" in QuestHold's settings: the backup file is encrypted with a password you choose, and you decide where it goes. Or turn on Cloud save, which keeps a copy in your own Google Play Games account (section 7).
  • On an iPhone, if you use iCloud Backup or back up the iPhone to a computer, iOS includes QuestHold's files and settings in that backup: your worlds (still encrypted), your paintings, your spoken notes and your settings. QuestHold keeps its AI models out of it; they download again after a restore. That backup is kept by Apple or on your computer, under Apple's privacy policy, never by us. To keep a copy you control, use "Back up and restore" in QuestHold's settings, as on Android.
  • Report emails are kept as section 9 says.
