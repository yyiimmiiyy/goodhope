Privacy Policy — QuestHold AI

Last updated: November 24, 2026

Provider: Goodhope Technologies LLC ("we", "us"), United States. Email: support@goodhopetechnologies.com. Our business address and phone number are shown on QuestHold's Google Play listing and, where the law of your country asks for it, on its App Store listing.

This policy covers QuestHold on Android phones and on iPhones (and iPads). Where the two work differently, it says so: "On Android" or "On an iPhone".

In short

  • QuestHold has no accounts and no servers of its own. We do not collect your personal information, and nothing you type or say is sent to us, except a report you choose to send, which goes to Goodhope's Firebase database (section 9).
  • The AI Game Master runs on your phone. Your worlds are stored on your phone, encrypted.
  • Google Play Games (on Android): while you are signed in, each deed you earn is also unlocked in your own Play Games account, and if you turn on Cloud save, a copy of each world you play goes to that account too. It goes to your Google account, never to us. On an iPhone, deeds unlock in your own Game Center account instead, and Cloud save, if you turn it on, keeps the copy in your own iCloud. It goes to your Apple Account, never to us (section 7).
  • QuestHold uses the internet to download its AI models, and to open a link or an email when you choose to. Google Play also checks your purchase and, where the law requires it, your age group. On an iPhone, the App Store checks your purchase, and Apple tells QuestHold your age range only if you choose to share it (section 6).
  • If you play co-op with friends, the phones at the table talk to each other over your Wi-Fi or hotspot. That goes from phone to phone, never to us.
  • No ads. QuestHold itself has no analytics, no tracking and no crash reports. On Android, the Google Play libraries inside the app (for purchases, Play Games and the model delivery) keep their own technical diagnostics with Google, under Google's privacy policy (sections 2 and 7). On an iPhone, the App Store, Game Center and iCloud are Apple's services, under Apple's privacy policy.

1. What stays on your phone

All of this is kept on your phone and is never sent to us. On an iPhone, iOS also puts QuestHold's files and settings (apart from the AI models) in your iPhone's own backup, if you use iCloud Backup or back up to a computer (section 12).

  • Your worlds: their places and people, your heroes, characters, the story so far, every dice roll and the chronicle. They are kept in an encrypted database (SQLCipher, AES-256) whose key is held in Android's secure key store on this phone. On an iPhone, the key is held in the iPhone's Keychain, Apple's secure store for keys.
  • What you type and say: your moves and anything else you write. In single-player, it leaves the phone only if you turn on Cloud save, which copies your worlds, moves included, to your own Google account, or on an iPhone to your own iCloud (section 7).
  • Your voice: if you play by voice, your speech is turned into text on the phone. See section 3.
  • Your voiceprint: if you play by voice, QuestHold learns your voice from your first spoken moves (or from three short lines you choose to say in Settings > Voice), so that at a shared table it can leave other voices, and a TV, out. It keeps a few numbers that describe your voice (its pitch and the shape of its sound), never a recording, only on this phone. QuestHold never sends them anywhere, not even to the other phones at your table. Turn this off or delete it at any time in Settings > Voice > Know my voice; uninstalling QuestHold deletes it too.
  • Your spoken notes: if you record a spoken note (on a place on the map, a person, or the Chronicle), the recording of your voice is kept on this phone, in QuestHold's own folder, with the words it says, so you can find it again. It goes into a backup file only if you make one, and on an iPhone into the iPhone's own backup if you use one (section 12). It never goes to the other phones at your table, into Cloud save, or to us. Delete a note in its list and the recording is deleted; delete the world and the recording is deleted with it when the world leaves Recently deleted (section 12).
  • Pictures: QuestHold's pictures (people, creatures, items and places) are drawn by the game's own 3D code on the phone, from parts made for QuestHold; no AI draws anything on your phone while you play. Some of those parts, and the paintings on QuestHold's own screens (backgrounds, boards and waiting pictures), were made before release with AI image and 3D tools (the screen paintings with FLUX.1 [schnell]; some of the 3D library's models were made or finished with AI tools). A photo you show the game for "Show it" is checked on the phone by a safety filter before it is used.
  • Settings: for example whether you roll your own dice, whether downloads wait for Wi-Fi, which version of the Terms you agreed to, which passages you reported, and your free-turn count (on Android, two numbers about your free trial are also kept by Google Play services: section 12).
  • Your age group from Google Play, or on an iPhone your age range from Apple, if they give one (section 6).
  • The AI model files the app downloaded, or, on Android, Google Play installed with it.
  • A short error log kept by the phone's own system log (Android's, or on an iPhone, iOS's), with personal details removed. QuestHold never sends it anywhere. If you chose, in your phone's settings, to share analytics with app developers (on an iPhone: Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements), Google or Apple may give us reports of QuestHold crashing: where in QuestHold's code it stopped, the phone model and the system version. They never hold your worlds or what you typed.

2. When QuestHold uses the internet

The model download

Google Play and the App Store bring only the app itself. Nothing else downloads until you agree in QuestHold's download question. Then, if you installed QuestHold from Google Play, Google Play delivers the story model (see "Models Google Play delivers" below), and QuestHold fetches only the other models your phone can run, from these places:

  • Story model (Gemma 4 E2B by Google): from Hugging Face, huggingface.co/unsloth/gemma-4-E2B-it-GGUF, only when Google Play does not deliver it (for example, an app not installed from Google Play, and always on an iPhone).
  • Story model for the graphics chip (Android, and an iPhone or iPad as the end of this item says; the same Gemma 4 E2B, as Google's LiteRT-LM file): from Hugging Face, huggingface.co/litert-community/gemma-4-E2B-it-litert-lm, only on phones whose graphics chip can run it, only after the story model already works on the phone and the other downloads are in, and only over Wi-Fi. If the phone's own test finds it is not faster there, the file is deleted. An iPhone or iPad downloads this file only to host on a phone with less memory (below): where its graphics chip is faster, it runs the story model above there.
  • Hosting on a phone with less memory (Android, iPhone and iPad): on a phone or iPad with at least 4 GB of memory but less than the 6 GB the full game needs, QuestHold may offer to host a lighter game. Only if you tap to try it, the phone downloads the story model for the graphics chip above (2.6 GB, from Hugging Face, only over Wi-Fi) and no other model, and then tests itself on the phone. If the test says no, the file is deleted and the phone plays as a guest at a friend's table. If it says no only for the moment (too little free memory, for example), the file stays so that you can test again a few times; after that the no is kept and the file is deleted.
  • Memory model (EmbeddingGemma 2 by Google): from Hugging Face, the files Google publishes as litert-community/embeddinggemma-2-740m-litert-lm, -text-vision-440m-litert-lm or -text-270m-litert-lm (the one this phone's kind uses).
  • Voices and speech recognition (Kokoro by hexgrad, with its Spanish voices, its pronunciation lists, Supertonic 2 by Supertone, Moonshine by Useful Sensors, Whisper by OpenAI for listening in Spanish, and Silero VAD): from Hugging Face (huggingface.co/onnx-community/Kokoro-82M-v1.0-ONNX, huggingface.co/Supertone/supertonic-2, huggingface.co/PeterReid/graphemes_to_phonemes_en_us, huggingface.co/moonshine-ai/moonshine, huggingface.co/ggerganov/whisper.cpp) and GitHub (github.com/hexgrad/misaki, github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx for the Silero VAD file).
  • Photo safety filter (for a photo you show the game): from Hugging Face, huggingface.co/onnx-community/nsfw_image_detection-ONNX.
  • The 3D library (the extra creatures, people, things and places QuestHold shows in your worlds; about 255 MB): on an iPhone or iPad, from Hugging Face, huggingface.co/GoodhopeTechnologies/questhold-3d-library, two files checked by their size and fingerprint before they are used. On Android, Google Play delivers it with the app (below). It holds 3D models and nothing personal, and nothing about you is sent with the request.

Versions before November 2026 also downloaded art models to paint pictures on the phone. QuestHold no longer paints: the first time a newer version starts, it deletes those models and the pictures they painted (your worlds, heroes and saves stay).

Like any download, these requests show Hugging Face, GitHub, Google Play and the content-delivery networks they use your IP address, the file being fetched and basic details of the app making the request. QuestHold sends no name, account, identifier or game data with them. Their own privacy policies apply: Hugging Face, GitHub, Google.

On Android, while the download runs, Android shows a notification so it can carry on when you leave the app. If Android stops a long download, QuestHold carries on with it the next time you open the app. On an iPhone, QuestHold asks iOS to carry on with the download while you are in another app, and iOS asks you once whether QuestHold may show notifications about it. If a download stops, QuestHold carries on with it the next time you open the app.

On Android, if another Goodhope Technologies app on your phone already holds the same model file, QuestHold can use that copy instead of downloading it again. Reports you send

You can report a passage the Game Master wrote (press and hold it). The report is made on your phone, the passage is hidden at once and stays hidden, and the report goes to Goodhope's database (Google's Cloud Firestore, a Firebase service we run, hosted by Google) so we can fix what went wrong. It contains:

  • the reason you picked and any details you typed;
  • the passage itself;
  • the app version, the language you play in, and the time (Google's server writes the time).

It holds no name, account, email address, device identifier or location, and it is not linked to you or your phone. Firebase starts only when you send your first report; QuestHold uses no Firebase Analytics or other Firebase service. If your phone has no signal, the report waits on the phone and goes when it can. Only if it cannot be delivered for several days, QuestHold offers to open your email app with the same report already written; nothing is sent by email unless you press send there. Section 9 explains what happens to a report we receive.

You can also report a world a friend sent you, on its preview or from its card in Worlds (press and hold it). One tap sends the report to Goodhope's database in the same way, removes the world from your phone (a world already on your phone goes to Recently deleted) and blocks the phone it came from; you can lift the block in Settings. The report contains the world's title, an id worked out from the world itself, the sender key the world came with (if it carried one: section 5), the world's id on your phone if you added it, and the time. QuestHold also shows our email address and the report as text you can copy and send from any email account.

The AI then runs on the host's phone, nothing is downloaded to the guest's phone, and the guest's moves go only to the phones at that table (section 5).
  • Speaking your moves: if you choose to play by voice, your phone asks you for the microphone first (an iPhone also asks for speech recognition). Your speech is turned into text on the phone, either by QuestHold's own speech model (Moonshine, or Whisper when you play in Spanish) or by your phone's own speech recognition, depending on what this phone has ready and does best; QuestHold always asks the phone's recognition to work on the phone only. The audio is not sent anywhere or kept (only the voiceprint numbers in section 1, if you leave that on, and a spoken note you choose to record). On Android, the phone's recognition is part of Android (usually provided by Google) and works under its own privacy terms. 4. Smart dice (Bluetooth)

If you choose to connect a smart die (such as Pixels dice), QuestHold uses Bluetooth to find the die and read the number it rolls. Android asks you for the "Nearby devices" permission first. On Android 11 and older, Android calls that permission "Location", because on those versions any Bluetooth scan could hint at where you are; QuestHold asks for it only to find your die, and never reads or keeps your location. On an iPhone, iOS asks you for Bluetooth first, and QuestHold never asks for your location. The connection is between your phone and your die only; nothing about it is sent to us.

5. Co-op on the same Wi-Fi or hotspot

Co-op works without the internet and without our servers. The phone that hosts the game runs the story; friends' phones join it over the same Wi-Fi network or the host's phone hotspot.

  • While a table has a free seat, the host's phone announces it on the local network every two seconds (UDP port 42021), so friends' phones can find it: a short table label the host chose (the host's hero's name unless they change it), how many seats are free, the port to connect to, and a random number for that table. Any device on the same network can see that announcement. It never carries the world's title, the story or anyone's moves.
  • To sit down, a friend's phone connects to the host's phone (TCP port 42020) and sends the four-digit table code shown on the host, and a random secret key that QuestHold made on the friend's phone, so a returning friend gets their own hero back. Nothing else is accepted before the code is right, and wrong guesses are limited.
  • Once seated, the friend's phone sends their hero (name, abilities and training), their moves (typed, or spoken and turned into text on their phone), the dice they roll, and their level-up choices. The host's phone sends each friend the story the Game Master tells, the heroes at the table and the scene, and asks them to roll when the game needs it.
  • Who receives it: only the phones at that table, directly over your local network. None of it goes to us or over the internet.
  • How it travels: as plain messages on your local network, not encrypted. Anyone else on the same network could see them if they tried, so play co-op on a network you trust, such as your home Wi-Fi or your own phone's hotspot, not public Wi-Fi.
  • What is kept: the world, including each friend's hero and their secret key, is saved only on the host's phone, in its encrypted database. A friend's phone keeps its own secret key and table settings.
  • On newer Android versions, Android asks you before QuestHold may use the local network. On an iPhone, iOS always asks you first. iOS may also keep an iPhone from sending or hearing the announcements in this section; a friend can then sit down from the table's QR code or invite link, or by typing its address.

Sending a world to a friend. You can send one of your worlds (as it was built, or as it stands, with your story so far, if you choose) to a friend:

  • To a phone nearby: while your phone offers the world, it announces it on the local network every two seconds (UDP port 42023): the world's title, the port to connect to and a random number. Any device on the same network can see that announcement. Your friend's phone connects to yours (TCP port 42022) with the four-digit code shown on your phone, and the world goes straight to it, checked so that nothing changed on the way. It travels as plain messages on your local network, like co-op. Leaving the screen stops offering it.
  • As a file: your phone's share sheet hands a world file to the app you pick (a message, an email, a drive). From there it is that app's to handle, under its own privacy policy.
  • What a world holds: its title, its places, people, words, laws and where its story starts. Never your hero, your story, your storyteller settings or your spoken notes, unless you choose to send the world as it stands, which holds your hero and the story so far (still never your spoken notes). It never goes to us.
  • The sender key: every world you send carries a random key that your phone makes once and keeps. It holds nothing about you; it is the same on every world you send. It is there so a friend who receives worlds from you can block worlds from your phone (the app stores require that a player can block someone who sends them abusive content), and we see it only if a friend puts it in a report they send us (section 2). Before the first world you send, QuestHold tells you this and you choose Share or Cancel; Cancel sends nothing. A world cannot be sent without the key. If a parent manages your account in Google Play and has not yet approved this change, sharing worlds waits until they do.
  • A world a friend sends you: before you add it, QuestHold reads its words on your phone with the same checks every story keeps. A world whose words cross the lines no story may cross (sexual content, harm to children, self-harm, hate, attacks on real people, strong language) cannot be added. Every word is read, the lines and veils a friend's campaign brings too: naming a topic is what they are for, but one with strong language, hate or an attack on a real person, one written as instructions to the storyteller, or one as long as a sentence, means the world cannot be added. Your own lines and veils are checked the same way before a world with its story leaves your phone; one that is too long opens in the storyteller's settings with its count showing, for you to shorten. Words about what a story may only tell offscreen (gore, torture, drugs and drunkenness) are hidden from its preview, and that world can still be added. You can report it (section 2) or block the phone it came from. Your phone keeps, only on this phone, the sender keys you blocked and which key each world you added came with; a backup of your worlds keeps it with them, so a restored world can still be reported and its phone blocked. Which key a world came with is deleted when the world is deleted for good. A key you blocked stays blocked until you unblock it: all at once in Settings > Worlds, or one phone at a time on the preview of a world that phone sends you.

Invite links and QR codes. A table's or a world's link (https://goodhopetechnologies.com/questhold/join/…) carries the host phone's address on your Wi-Fi and the four-digit code after the "#", which a browser never sends to any server. Opening it on a phone without QuestHold AI shows a page on goodhopetechnologies.com (the site's host sees the page request, as for any web page). If you tap "Get QuestHold AI on Google Play" there, the address and code go to Google Play as the install referrer, and QuestHold AI reads them back on the phone once, on its first start, to take you to the table. QuestHold AI on an iPhone never reads an install referrer: there, scan the table's code or type its address inside QuestHold AI.

6. Age checks (Google Play Age Signals, and Apple's Declared Age Range on an iPhone)

Some places, such as Texas, require app stores and apps to know whether a player is a minor and to get a parent's approval for some things.

On Android, when you open QuestHold, and when you open the Pro screen, QuestHold asks Google Play's Age Signals service on the phone what it knows about the account: whether its age is shared, its age range (for example 13 to 15), and whether a parent manages it. Google Play may ask you first whether to share it. QuestHold keeps only that answer, on your phone, and uses it only to follow these laws:

  • If a parent manages the account, buying Pro goes through Google Play's parental approval, and Pro opens when the parent approves.
  • If Google Play says the account belongs to someone under 18 whom no parent manages in Google Play, Pro waits for a parent: they can buy it on their own account, or supervise yours and approve it.
  • If the law where you live requires a verified age and Google Play does not have one yet, Pro waits until you confirm your age in Google Play.
  • If Google Play says the account belongs to someone under 13, QuestHold cannot be played on it.

On an iPhone (iOS 26 or later), QuestHold asks Apple's Declared Age Range when you open QuestHold (after the first set-up) and again when you open the Pro screen. Apple shows its own screen, and you (or your parent) choose whether to share. If you share, QuestHold receives only an age range (for example 13 to 15) and whether a parent or guardian declared it. QuestHold keeps only that answer, on your phone, and uses it only for these rules:

  • If a parent or guardian declared the age, buying Pro asks them to approve it with Apple's Ask to Buy, and Pro opens when they do.
  • If the range is under 18 and no parent or guardian declared it, Pro waits for a parent: they can buy it on their own account, or approve it with Ask to Buy.
  • If the range is under 13, QuestHold cannot be played on that account.

Free play is never held back by these checks, and if Google Play or Apple gives no answer (or you choose not to share), QuestHold works as normal. We never receive the answer and never use it for anything else.

7. Google Play Games and Game Center: sign-in, achievements and cloud save

On Android, QuestHold works with Google Play Games, the Google service that keeps game achievements and saved games in your own Google account. If you use Google Play Games on this phone, it signs you in by itself when QuestHold opens; you can sign in, sign out or turn it off in Google Play Games.

  • Deeds (achievements): QuestHold's deeds are earned on your phone from what happens in your games, and are kept on your phone. While you are signed in to Google Play Games, each deed you earn is also unlocked in your Play Games account, where you (and anyone who can see your Play Games profile) can see it. Only which deed it is goes with it: no story, no moves and no game data.
  • Cloud save, off until you turn it on: if you turn on Cloud save in QuestHold's settings, a copy of each world you play (its places and people, the story so far and its dice rolls) is kept as a saved game in your own Play Games account, when you stop playing and when you tap Save now. For a world a friend sent you, the copy also keeps which world it is and the sender key it came with (section 5), so a world you bring back can still be reported and its phone blocked. It goes from your phone to your Google account: we never receive it and cannot read it. You can bring a copy back on any phone signed in to the same account. The copy is sealed so QuestHold can tell it was not changed or cut short; Google keeps it under Google's privacy policy.
  • When copies disagree: a save never replaces a copy of the same world that went further on another phone, and bringing a copy back never replaces a story on your phone: it comes in beside it. If two phones save at the same moment, Google Play keeps the one with more turns played.
  • Deleting it: turn Cloud save off in QuestHold's settings to stop saving. Copies already saved stay in your Play Games account until you delete QuestHold's data or your Play Games profile in Google Play Games' settings.

While you use it, the Play Games library inside QuestHold also sends Google its own analytics and diagnostics, which Google uses to keep its service working (developer.android.com/games/pgs/data-collection). Google Play Games is Google's service, under Google's privacy policy: Google.

On an iPhone, QuestHold works with Apple's Game Center instead. If you are signed in to Game Center on your iPhone, QuestHold is signed in when it opens; if you are not, Apple's sign-in shows only when you tap Sign in in QuestHold's settings. You can turn Game Center off in your iPhone's Settings.

  • Deeds (achievements): earned and kept on your phone, as on Android. While you are signed in, each deed you earn is also unlocked in your Game Center account, where you (and other players, as your Game Center privacy settings allow) can see it. Only which deed it is goes with it: no story, no moves and no game data.
  • Who is signed in: Game Center tells QuestHold your Game Center name, which QuestHold shows in its settings, and a player ID, which QuestHold keeps on the phone to remember which deeds it has already sent and which worlds it has already saved.
  • Cloud save, off until you turn it on: if you turn on Cloud save in QuestHold's settings, a copy of each world you play (the same copy as on Android: its places and people, the story so far and its dice rolls, and for a world a friend sent you, which world it is and the sender key it came with) is kept as a Game Center saved game in your own iCloud, when you stop playing and when you tap Save now. It needs iCloud Drive turned on. It goes from your iPhone to your iCloud: we never receive it and cannot read it. You can bring a copy back on any iPhone or iPad signed in to the same Apple Account. The copy is sealed so QuestHold can tell it was not changed or cut short; Apple keeps it under Apple's privacy policy.
  • When copies disagree: as on Android, a save never replaces a copy of the same world that went further on another device, and bringing a copy back never replaces a story on your iPhone: it comes in beside it. If two devices saved the same world, QuestHold keeps the copy with more turns played.
  • Deleting it: turn Cloud save off in QuestHold's settings to stop saving. Copies already saved stay in your iCloud until you delete QuestHold's data from your iCloud storage in your iPhone's Settings (your name, then iCloud).

Game Center and iCloud are Apple's services, under Apple's privacy policy: Apple.

8. World news notifications, off until you turn them on

After your second session QuestHold asks you once whether you want word when your world moves; you can also turn World news on or off in its settings. If you say yes, your phone asks whether QuestHold may show notifications. Then, at most once a week, when a group in your world moves on while you are away, QuestHold shows one short notification about it. The AI Game Master writes it on your phone ahead of time and the phone schedules it: nothing is sent over the internet and nothing reaches us. It never makes a sound between 10 PM and 8 AM. On Android, the notification itself has a button that turns World news off; on an iPhone, turn it off in QuestHold's settings or in the iPhone's notification settings.

  • Nearby devices (Bluetooth): only if you connect a smart die (section 4). Android asks first. On Android 11 and older this permission is called Location (section 4).
  • Camera: only when you tap Scan to read a friend's QR code (to join their table or take a world they share), or take a photo for "Show it" when you start a story. Android asks first. The QR code is read on your phone; no picture is kept or sent anywhere.
  • Physical activity: only for shake to roll, so walking, or riding in a car or bus, never rolls your dice. QuestHold asks once, the first time a die waits for a shake (never when you first open the app), and says why before Android asks. If you allow it, QuestHold reads the phone's step detector, and Google Play services' activity recognition (which tells whether the phone is in a vehicle), only while a die waits for a shake. These readings are used on your phone only, kept only in QuestHold's memory while it runs, never saved and never sent anywhere. If you refuse, shaking to roll still works. On Android 9, Android asks nothing for these readings, and QuestHold uses them in the same way.

On an iPhone, iOS asks you before each of these, the first time QuestHold needs it:

  • Local network: only for co-op and for sending a world to a phone nearby (section 5).
  • Notifications: for notices about a model download, and for World news if you turn it on (section 8).

QuestHold asks once, the first time a die waits for a shake (never when you first open the app), and says why before iOS asks. If you allow it, QuestHold reads the iPhone's step counter and its motion activity (which tells whether you are walking or in a vehicle) while a die waits for a shake, with the last few minutes before it, so a ride that just ended still counts. These readings are used on your phone only, kept only in QuestHold's memory while it runs, never saved and never sent anywhere. If you refuse, shaking to roll still works.

Shake to roll also reads the phone's motion sensors (its accelerometer and gyroscope), which need no permission, only while a die waits for a shake, to tell a shake from the phone's other movements. They are read on your phone only, never kept and never sent anywhere.

QuestHold never reads your location, and does not ask for your contacts. A QR picture or a "Show it" photo you pick from your phone's photos is chosen in the phone's own photo picker (Android's, or on an iPhone Apple's), which hands QuestHold only the pictures you pick (up to three for "Show it"), so QuestHold never asks to see your photo library. The copy the picker hands over is deleted from the phone as soon as it is read, with any other copy the picker left in QuestHold's storage (and anything left by a read that was cut off is deleted the next time QuestHold starts). A "Show it" photo is read on your phone only, and never stored or sent: the photo safety filter checks it first, a photo that is mostly a person's face is not used, and the people in a photo are left out, so only words about the place and the things in it reach your story. While you start the story, a small copy of each photo shows beside its words, held only in memory and cleared when the story begins, when you leave that screen or when QuestHold goes to the background.

12. Keeping and deleting your data

  • Your worlds stay on your phone until you delete them (a deleted world first waits in Recently deleted, below). Uninstalling QuestHold, or clearing its storage in Android's settings, deletes them and everything else QuestHold stored, apart from two numbers about your free trial (below). We cannot recover them. On an iPhone, deleting the app deletes your worlds and QuestHold's files, but iOS keeps the few small entries QuestHold put in the Keychain (the database key, your Pro record, and your trial and free-turn counts), and QuestHold uses them again if you install it again.
  • Recently deleted: when you delete a world, it moves to Recently deleted, still whole on your phone (its story, pictures and spoken notes stay as they were), and you can bring it back from there for 30 days. Once its 30 days are over, QuestHold deletes it from the phone the next time you open Worlds or Recently deleted (a few at a time); Delete forever deletes it at once. A backup file you make leaves out the worlds in Recently deleted.
  • On Android, so that the 7-day trial does not start over when QuestHold's storage is cleared or the app is reinstalled, QuestHold also keeps two numbers in Google Play services' Block Store: when you first opened QuestHold, and the latest time it has seen on your phone's clock. Nothing else goes there: no worlds, nothing you typed or said, no name or account details. Google Play services keeps them on your phone, where only QuestHold can read them, and, if your phone backs up to your Google account (Settings > Google > Backup), in that backup too, end-to-end encrypted when your phone has a screen lock, so they also come back when you set up a new phone from that backup. We never receive them, and you do not sign in to anything in QuestHold for this. Google keeps and deletes that backup under Google's privacy policy.
  • On Android, QuestHold does not let Android back up its files to your Google account. To keep a copy of a world, use "Back up and restore" in QuestHold's settings: the backup file is encrypted with a password you choose, and you decide where it goes. Or turn on Cloud save, which keeps a copy in your own Google Play Games account (section 7).
  • On an iPhone, if you use iCloud Backup or back up the iPhone to a computer, iOS includes QuestHold's files and settings in that backup: your worlds (still encrypted), your spoken notes and your settings. QuestHold keeps its AI models out of it; they download again after a restore. That backup is kept by Apple or on your computer, under Apple's privacy policy, never by us. To keep a copy you control, use "Back up and restore" in QuestHold's settings, as on Android. Or turn on Cloud save, which keeps a copy in your own iCloud (section 7).
  • Reports, and report emails, are kept as section 9 says.
