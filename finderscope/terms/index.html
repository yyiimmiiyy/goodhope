Sky positions, rise and set times, forecasts, maps, skylines and dark-spot suggestions are guides for planning a night under the stars. They are not a navigation tool and not a safety tool. Do not rely on Finderscope for backcountry travel, emergencies or any decision where a mistake could hurt someone. Finderscope says nothing about whether you may enter a place: check land access, park hours and local rules with the people responsible before you go.

The camera sky view names what the phone's compass and sensors say you are pointing at, and those are often off by a few degrees. The eyepiece camera says a star field is identified only when its check passes, and it can still be wrong.

6. Your safety

  • Never look at the Sun through a telescope, binoculars or a finder, or with your eyes alone, without a certified solar filter made for that purpose. Permanent eye damage can happen instantly, with no pain. Before a telescope mount points near the Sun, or while the Sun is up, Finderscope warns you and asks you to confirm; that warning does not replace your own care. Keep filters on, and caps on finders, whenever the Sun could come into view.
  • A GoTo mount moves on its own. Before you start a slew, make sure no person, pet, cable, chair or part of the telescope can be struck or caught, and stay with the mount while it moves. Use Finderscope's Stop button, or your mount's own controls or power switch, if anything looks wrong. Finderscope sends your mount the standard commands of its command set; how the mount responds, including its limits and alignment, is up to the mount. Follow your mount maker's instructions.
  • Observing happens outdoors, at night, often far from help. You are responsible for your own safety, your equipment and your awareness of your surroundings: dress for the cold, tell someone where you are going, watch for traffic, wildlife and uneven ground, and keep a light (a red one keeps your night vision) for moving around.
  • Do not use Finderscope while driving.
